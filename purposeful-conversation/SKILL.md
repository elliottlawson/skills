---
name: purposeful-conversation
description: Start and drive an outcome-oriented conversation with prescribed ownership. Use when the user hands over an area or objective to work through together — design, planning, decisions, process work — and expects the agent to own the thread, keep it structured, and drive it to a documented resolution. Trigger phrases: "let's work through X", "help me sort out X", "I want your help thinking through X", or any hand-off of an area with a goal.
---

The user has handed you an area and an objective. The objective is now yours. You are not a consultant waiting for direction — you own the thread and drive it to resolution. They steer; you move the work forward.

## 1. Ground before opining

Before the first substantive turn: orient in the workspace, then map what actually exists in the area — read the relevant project docs, dispatch an explore sub-agent over the codebase if the work touches real systems. Every proposal you make later must anchor to something real: an existing feature, a live column, a shipped decision. No floating abstractions. If you catch yourself proposing something without knowing what exists today, stop and go look first.

## 2. Run every turn in the fixed format

Each substantive turn uses a fixed discuss template (your formatting skill's DISCUSS mode, if you have one), with discipline:

```
Topic: [the narrow subject of this turn]

My read: [synthesis of what the user said + your position, grounded in what exists.
         Mark settled points explicitly: "Locked."]

Open:
1. [narrow, answerable-now question]
2. [narrow, answerable-now question]
```

- One screen max.
- The Open questions drive the next turn. Number them, keep them narrow, give your lean on each when you have one.
- Questions must narrow over time. If Open grows instead of shrinks, you are diverging — stop and re-ground.

## 3. Convergence is the goal

Track two counts as you go: decisions locked, questions open. Close sections loudly ("Tool overload — closed"). A locked decision never re-opens unless the user reopens it. Done means: zero open questions, documented, committed. When you get there, say so plainly: "Settled — N decisions, zero open questions."

Direction alone is not done. Keep going until the outputs are concrete — actual fields, actual flows, actual screens. "What would execution look like?" gets answered before the thread closes.

## 4. Serve the outcome, not your proposal

When the user's pushback dissolves one of your ideas, drop it and say so — "your examples just dissolved it" is a valid and welcome turn. Never defend a position because it was yours. Generalize every correction immediately: apply it to the whole artifact, not just the instance where it came up.

## 5. Document continuously

Write the artifact early — a design doc, decision record, or the project's equivalent — and update it at each milestone. Commit and push after every update. Be generous: a fresh session must be able to pick up exactly where you left off from the doc alone. Record session outcomes in the project's history log as you go.

## 6. Feedback lands the same turn

Style and process corrections apply from your very next turn. Log load-bearing ones through your self-improvement process (if you have one) in a background sub-agent and keep working.

## Boundaries

- If you have a response-formatting skill with a DISCUSS mode, build on it — same template, plus ownership and convergence discipline. Do not invent a new format.
- Distinct from a grilling skill (that stress-tests the user's plan by interrogating it) and an alignment skill (that resolves ambiguity about the goal itself). If the objective handed to you is genuinely unclear, align first — then start this skill.
